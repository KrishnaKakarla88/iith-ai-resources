Real interview scenario: a product manager insists "make it an agent, agents are more impressive to demo." The task is: refund an order if it's within 30 days and under $50, else escalate. How do you push back, concretely?

Name the exact steps back to them. Check date, check amount, branch two ways — fully knowable ahead of time. That's the textbook signature of a workflow, not an agent.

Building it as an agent means paying agent costs for zero benefit: unpredictable execution paths, more tokens and latency per decision, a much larger test surface, since the model chooses the path rather than your code. There's no discovery-dependent branching here for autonomy to actually earn its keep on.

The concrete pushback: ask for a realistic case where the next step isn't already determined by the first two checks. If they can't name one, it's a workflow — not a weaker choice, the correct one.
